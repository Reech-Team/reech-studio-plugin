# Make · Building in the creator format

Part of the `make` skill: everything there (the order of work, the look, the checks, delivery) still holds.

**Text looks are the person's choice** (format `creator` today; see `TextLook`/`CaptionLook` in `src/components/text-look.ts`). Ask what they like, or match their reference video, and never tell them a look is impossible before trying these:
- `captions: { preset, base, active, key, keys, words, y, width, enter }`. Presets: `bold` (default: all caps, black outline, the word being said in the accent), `editorial` (white sans in sentence case, key words in a bigger cream serif italic on their own line, words appear as said, no boxes), `clean`, `boxed` (one dark block behind the line) and `none` (no captions, for finishing in CapCut or Edits). Any field overrides the preset: `base` styles every word (`font`, `weight`, `italic`, `size`, `color`, `case: "upper"|"lower"|"none"`, `tracking`, `stroke`, `shadow: "none"|"soft"|"hard"`, `box`), `active` the word being said, `key` the key words (plus `scale` and `ownLine`), `keys` lists which words are key (regexes for whole words, e.g. `["per[uú]", "diez", "patria"]`: choose them from what matters in the narration), `words` sets words per page (1–6), `y` the top of the block, `enter` the animation (`pop`, `rise`, `fade`, `word`, `none`).
- Titles (`hook`, `texts`): `titles: "box"|"plain"|"editorial"`, `titleLook` overrides for all of them, `look` on a single one; `*word*` inside a title takes `titleKey`'s look, and `\n` breaks the line.
- Fonts: `"sans"` (Montserrat) and `"serif"` (Playfair Display), both with italics, are always loaded; `"display"`/`"body"` are the brand's. Any Google font: load it in `config.ts` with `import * as Anton from "@remotion/google-fonts/Anton"; import { googleFont } from "<../ to repo root>/src/brand-fonts"; const anton = googleFont(Anton, [400], { italic: true });` and use `anton.fontFamily`. The brand's own font file: the person uploads it (TTF, OTF, WOFF) with the upload page to `fonts/`, then `fontFile("Name", "brands/<slug>/fonts/<file>")` from the same module.
- A recorded clip's sound: `media_clean` the clip and set `voice` to its `.clean.wav`, so the video carries the cleaned voice instead of the raw microphone.
When the brand has `system.json → textLook`, start from it. When the person settles on a look, save it there (`brand_save → system.textLook`: the `captions`, `titles`, `titleLook` and `titleKey` they chose, `keys` left out because they change per video) so the next video starts from it.

**Recreating a reference** (format `creator`; types in `src/components/scene-model.ts`, `file_get` it). Work from the reference's `components.json` (plan skill): every component gets built, entry by entry, with these blocks, all switched on a spoken word (`at: { re: "^word" }`) or a second. Rectangles are fractions of the frame (`{ x, y, w, h }` from 0 to 1), the same numbers `media_analyze` measured, so paste them.
- `layouts: [{ at, picture: "full" | rect, background, move, focus, zoom, radius, border, panel }]`: where the footage sits. A box on a color (`"#FDFDFD"`), a gradient, `"image:<path>"` or `"pattern:<path>"` (tiled, `patternSize`); `move: 0.3` slides or scales it from the previous layout instead of a hard cut; `focus: [x, y]` keeps the face in a cropped box; `panel: { src, rect, fit }` puts a second picture beside it (split screen); a band on top is a `picture` starting lower (`{ x: 0, y: 0.2, w: 1, h: 0.8 }`) on a color.
- `phrases: [{ at, to?, text, x, y, w, align, build, look, styles, behind }]`: designed text anywhere. `\n` breaks lines, `build: "word"` (as said, default) | `"line"` | `"all"`; `*word*` takes the `key` style (bigger cream serif italic), `{accent:words}` cream, `{under:words}` underlined, `{hollow:words}` outline only, or any style you name in `styles` (each a text look plus `scale`). `phraseLook` and `styles` at the top level set them for every phrase. With phrases, automatic captions are off unless `captions` is set.
- **Highlights (the vocabulary a style ficha names with letters):** A word by word is `build: "word"`; **B** `{marker:words}` paints the accent band behind them, left to right, as each is said (text turns paper); **C** a punch is `*key*` with `look.motion.punch`; **D** `{strike:words}` draws a line through them and dims them at `strikeAt` (a spoken word; default: with the stamp, else just after each is said), and `stamp: { text, at? }` on the phrase slams a seal under it (paper chip, outlined, rotated; turns ink when the warning color wouldn't read); **E** `labels: [{ at, text, point: [x, y] | "face", side? }]`: a dot on the thing, a line that draws, the tag beside it (it flips side or goes above when there's no room); **F** `equations: [{ at, left, op: "=" | "→" | "≈" | "≠" | "+" | "vs", right, y? }]`: left lands on `at`, the operator draws just before `right`, which lands on its own spoken word (found after `at`; `rightAt` to choose); long pairs stack; **G** counters are a layer with `sounds`. Each lands with its own sound (strike `tac-tachon`, stamp `tac-hit`, label and operator the look's `enter`; `sound: null` silences one) and counts in DESIGN-11. A `mark` on any style you define (`"marker"` or `"strike"`, `markColor`) makes it one of these.
- `grids` for collages (4a above); `media: [{ src, at, to?, rect, enter, wiggle, layer }]` only for a single sticker, GIF or clip next to a word (`layer: "top"`).
- `cards: [{ at, to?, text, rect, background, color, look, enter, rotate, layer }]`: a designed tile with a word or two (default serif italic on cream), popping in like a photo; the placeholder when a collage has no images yet (plan skill: ask, then stock, then cards).
- `behind: true` on a phrase puts it behind the person. For each range where text sits behind them, `media_cutout` (brand, the clip, `from`, `to` in clip seconds, at most 20 s each and about 6 s of work per second; portrait matting on our CPU, nothing per video) writes `<clip>.cutout-<from>-<to>.webm`; list them as `cutouts: [{ file, from, to }]`. The renderer places a behind phrase so that enough of it shows (it moves up to peek over the head when the person would swallow it, and draws it in front when even that fails), so write it where it reads best and check the still at 100%: hair edges and anything held in front (a cushion, a mic) must read right. Without a cutout the text draws in front.
- **Anything else** (a drawing that draws itself, a custom chart, a new kind of motion): write it for this brand as `brands/<slug>/components/<name>.tsx` with `file_put` (a React component; `useCurrentFrame()` counts from its own start; only `remotion`, `react` and `src/` imports), export it from the video's `config.ts` as `export const components = { <name>: Component }` (import it from `"../../../../components/<name>"`), and place it with `layers: [{ at, to?, component: "<name>", props, rect, layer }]`. Log it with `log_append` as a library candidate so the team can make it a shared block.
- **Check it against the reference before the render:** `video_stills` with `times` at the moments of each component (the reference's dense-sheet and change-strip times, mapped to this video), then compare them next to the reference frames, component by component, and fix what differs. Name anything still not exact to the person before the render, never after.

## 4b. The toolkit: fast, professional talking-head edits (format `creator`)

One camera can look like a produced edit. In the `creator` config, `camera` turns the picked treatment into shots (`src/components/toolkit.ts`; `library_search toolkit`):
- `rhythm` from `pace.cuts_per_min`: under 15 → `"none"` (one shot per take) or `{ mode: "phrase", every: 3 }`; 15–30 → `"phrase"` (a new shot about every 2.2 s, cut just before a word); 30–45 → `"beat"` when the video has `beats` (cuts on a beat at a word start), else `"steady"`; over 45 → `{ mode: "steady", every: 1 }`. Cuts never fall inside a word and never skip footage: the voice is untouched.
- `angles`: the cycle the shots go through, from the treatment's framings (`wide`, `medium`, `close`, `tight`, `left`, `right`, `tilt`, or `{ zoom, x, y, rotate }`); two shots in a row never share one. `shots: [{ at, angle, move }]` pins a moment (the turn on a `tight` with `shake`, the offer on a `close`). `move` (default `push` on shots of 2.5 s or more): `push`, `pull`, `drift`, `shake`, `none`.
- `transitions: [{ at, kind, dir? }]` from the treatment's transitions: `whip` (with `dir`), `zoom`, `flash`, `glitch`, `blur`, `dip`; it lands on the cut nearest `at` or makes one at that word. Use them where the story turns, not on every cut: a transition with no reason looks like a template.
- `sound` from `sound.density`: `none`, `light` (each transition gets its sound), `medium` (plus every other angle change), `dense` (a hit on every change). The planner keeps them spaced and under the voice, and context sounds (`cues`) win.
- B-roll `rate` (0.25–4) slows or speeds a cutaway; never the person talking.
- Look at the stills **around each transition** and a proxy with sound: a whip or a glitch only shows in motion. The format keeps every move inside the frame (no black edges).

## 4c. The motion kit: the effects of an advanced edit (format `creator`)

Nine library layers any video places by name in `layers` (no `components` export; `library_search "motion kit"`, then `file_get` an entry's manifest for its props). Each is brand-neutral and takes its colors from props: color them with the brand. They are what made a laptop brand's reel and a creator's video read as high-end on 2026-11-14:
- **Story devices:** `GlassNotification` (a phone notification that slides down: meetings, reminders, "listo ✓") and `LiveCard` (a live device card with a pulsing dot and a sound wave: a baby monitor, a call; repeat it with a new status to tell a night or a process). Keep them off the face: top in wide shots, bottom (`y` about 0.74) in close-ups. One at a time, each with its sound cue.
- **Big words:** `SlideWords` (slides across while said, motion blur) and `DropWords` (letters drop in, a drawn underline). For the hook and one or two key phrases, not every line; the small captions spread left and right carry the rest (`phrases` with `x`, `w`, `align`, `build: "all"`, `enter: "none"`).
- **Transitions with a reason:** `PanelWipe` (the next shot in vertical panels) and `CircleReveal` (the next shot opens in a circle). At the turn or the reveal, with a whoosh or sparkle; they cover the picture with their clip while the voice continues (`layer: "over-picture"`).
- **Time passing:** `ClockSweep` (hands sweep `fromH` → `toH`, the face goes from night to morning).
- **Grade that tells the story:** `MoodGrade` (teal shadows, warm highlights, grain, vignette) or `NightGrade` (night with a glow around lights; `morning: true` for the answer). Put them on `layer: "over-picture"` so text stays clean, set `brand: { ...brand, style: "clean" }`, and change grade at the turn (two layers that meet there).
- **Framing:** a person far from the camera needs framings of their own: `angles` and `shots` take `{ zoom, x, y, rotate }` around the point kept in place (a 4K full-body take: face about `{ zoom: 2.6, x: 0.5, y: 0.2 }`, chest `{ zoom: 1.85, y: 0.27 }`). Without them every shot looks the same.
- Check the stills around each layer: a card over a face or a word under a card only shows there.
