# Make: library first, then preview, render and check

Needs a `script.json` from `plan` (`video_get` shows it). Follow the recipe that fits the source material (`library_search` with `type: recipe`; the usual one is `short-form-brand-video`). Prefer quality over speed: look at frames, run the checks, and fix before the final render. Say you're on it with `lock_acquire` on the video folder.

## 1. Library first: reuse, tune, extend

`library_search` for formats, styles, components and media. Then:
- **Reuse** an entry as is when it fits the concept.
- **Tune** it for one brand when only sizes, motion speed, captions, SFX level or punch-ins differ: `tune` in the brand's `brand.ts` (`file_get` / `file_put`), no new code.
- **Extend or create** a format, style or component: that's a Reech-side step (new library code is reviewed and released by the team). Describe exactly what the piece needs and ask the team; meanwhile use the closest library entry so the rest of the work continues.

## 2. Audio

Everything downstream reads **one timed transcript**: word timings for captions, cuts and the audit.

- **Generated voice** (organizations with ElevenLabs; `me` → flags.eleven): `video_audio` on the video. It uses `script.json → voice` if the plan chose one, otherwise the brand's calibrated voice, then finishes the audio like an editor would (tightens pauses, caps the speed-up by the style's energy, voice chain, a music bed at the video's exact length, carved so the voice sits on top) and writes `media/brands/<slug>/audio/<video>/voices.json` with word timings and measured levels. Listen to it through `media_links`. An organization using its own ElevenLabs account pays for these characters there, so write the final script before generating. If the voice isn't found, it isn't on that account: the job's error lists the voices it has; pick the closest fit, update `script.json` or the profile, and say which you used. The result's `voiceCheck` transcribes the voice-over and runs the final QA's transcript and pause checks on it: when it fails, fix the script line it names (or a word the voice skips) and generate again now, before any stills; a render with a voice-over that fails here fails QA too. If the result has `musicError`, the account refused the music: tell the person and use their own track or none. If the video runs over its target length at the style's speed cap, cut words from the script and run it again; don't raise the speed.
- **Recorded voice** (every organization): the person uploads the recording (`media_upload_page` with `folder: "audio/<video>"`), then `media_clean` removes the background noise and enhances the voice into `<name>.clean.wav` (always do it, unless they say the sound is already finished), and `media_transcribe` gives word timings, speech segments and speakers next to the file. Use the `.clean.wav` in the edit. Cut on that transcript. Don't speed it up or re-voice it. In `config.ts`, import the words instead of copying them: `import words from "../../../../../../../public/brands/<slug>/<folder>/<name>.words.json";`. Transcribers mishear brand names: the job corrects close spellings of the brand's name and reports them in `brandNameCorrected`; check any other name and write the brand correctly in `script.json`.
- **Music:** the brand's own tracks or shared library music (`library_search`, `type: media`); `media_beats` on the track (with `campaign` + `video`) writes the video's `beats.json` for cutting on the beat. The edit sets music and SFX levels from the measured loudness (`media_stats` on the audio folder writes `levels.json`): music 14 dB under the voice, +5 dB in pauses; SFX ≥ 8 dB under the voice, at most one per 2 s. Music must have mid-register parts (strings, piano, pads, plucks); `media_stats` warns when a track is mostly sub-bass, which disappears on phones. Never end on silence.
- **Footage audio:** generated or stock clips often come with sound, sometimes speech in another language. Mute it in the edit (`script.json → footage_audio: "muted"`, list the files in `footage`) and add the ambience you want on purpose.

## 3. Footage

Use the brand's own media first (`media_list`), then the shared library footage (`library_search`, `type: media`), then stock the person provides, then generated media they bring (the person uploads; `media_upload_page`). Brand-owned media stays under `media/brands/<slug>/` and is listed in `assets/index.json` with source and license. Run `media_stats` on new footage so the edit knows how each clip moves and its color (no digital push on moving footage, shots matched in color).

**Talking to camera** (creator, UGC, presenter, testimonial): best is a real person recording on their phone (transcribe it, cut on the transcript, format `creator`). Lip-synced AI presenters are a Reech-side production step for now; ask the team.

**Text looks are the person's choice** (format `creator` today; see `TextLook`/`CaptionLook` in `src/components/text-look.ts`). Ask what they like, or match their reference video, and never tell them a look is impossible before trying these:
- `captions: { preset, base, active, key, keys, words, y, width, enter }`. Presets: `bold` (default: all caps, black outline, the word being said in the accent), `editorial` (white sans in sentence case, key words in a bigger cream serif italic on their own line, words appear as said, no boxes), `clean`, `boxed` (one dark block behind the line) and `none` (no captions, for finishing in CapCut or Edits). Any field overrides the preset: `base` styles every word (`font`, `weight`, `italic`, `size`, `color`, `case: "upper"|"lower"|"none"`, `tracking`, `stroke`, `shadow: "none"|"soft"|"hard"`, `box`), `active` the word being said, `key` the key words (plus `scale` and `ownLine`), `keys` lists which words are key (regexes for whole words, e.g. `["per[uú]", "diez", "patria"]`: choose them from what matters in the narration), `words` sets words per page (1–6), `y` the top of the block, `enter` the animation (`pop`, `rise`, `fade`, `word`, `none`).
- Titles (`hook`, `texts`): `titles: "box"|"plain"|"editorial"`, `titleLook` overrides for all of them, `look` on a single one; `*word*` inside a title takes `titleKey`'s look, and `\n` breaks the line.
- Fonts: `"sans"` (Montserrat) and `"serif"` (Playfair Display), both with italics, are always loaded; `"display"`/`"body"` are the brand's. Any Google font: load it in `config.ts` with `import * as Anton from "@remotion/google-fonts/Anton"; import { googleFont } from "<../ to repo root>/src/brand-fonts"; const anton = googleFont(Anton, [400], { italic: true });` and use `anton.fontFamily`. The brand's own font file: the person uploads it (TTF, OTF, WOFF) with the upload page to `fonts/`, then `fontFile("Name", "brands/<slug>/fonts/<file>")` from the same module.
- A recorded clip's sound: `media_clean` the clip and set `voice` to its `.clean.wav`, so the video carries the cleaned voice instead of the raw microphone.
When the brand has `system.json → textLook`, start from it. When the person settles on a look, save it there (`brand_save → system.textLook`: the `captions`, `titles`, `titleLook` and `titleKey` they chose, `keys` left out because they change per video) so the next video starts from it.

**Recreating a reference** (format `creator`; types in `src/components/scene-model.ts`, `file_get` it). Work from the reference's `components.json` (plan skill): every component gets built, entry by entry, with these blocks, all switched on a spoken word (`at: { re: "^word" }`) or a second. Rectangles are fractions of the frame (`{ x, y, w, h }` from 0 to 1), the same numbers `media_analyze` measured, so paste them.
- `layouts: [{ at, picture: "full" | rect, background, move, focus, zoom, radius, border, panel }]`: where the footage sits. A box on a color (`"#FDFDFD"`), a gradient, `"image:<path>"` or `"pattern:<path>"` (tiled, `patternSize`); `move: 0.3` slides or scales it from the previous layout instead of a hard cut; `focus: [x, y]` keeps the face in a cropped box; `panel: { src, rect, fit }` puts a second picture beside it (split screen); a band on top is a `picture` starting lower (`{ x: 0, y: 0.2, w: 1, h: 0.8 }`) on a color.
- `phrases: [{ at, to?, text, x, y, w, align, build, look, styles, behind }]`: designed text anywhere. `\n` breaks lines, `build: "word"` (as said, default) | `"line"` | `"all"`; `*word*` takes the `key` style (bigger cream serif italic), `{accent:words}` cream, `{under:words}` underlined, `{hollow:words}` outline only, or any style you name in `styles` (each a text look plus `scale`). `phraseLook` and `styles` at the top level set them for every phrase. With phrases, automatic captions are off unless `captions` is set.
- `grids` for collages (4a above); `media: [{ src, at, to?, rect, enter, wiggle, layer }]` only for a single sticker, GIF or clip next to a word (`layer: "top"`).
- `cards: [{ at, to?, text, rect, background, color, look, enter, rotate, layer }]`: a designed tile with a word or two (default serif italic on cream), popping in like a photo; the placeholder when a collage has no images yet (plan skill: ask, then stock, then cards).
- `behind: true` on a phrase puts it behind the person. For each range where text sits behind them, `media_cutout` (brand, the clip, `from`, `to` in clip seconds, at most 60 s each; portrait matting on our CPU, nothing per video) writes `<clip>.cutout-<from>-<to>.webm`; list them as `cutouts: [{ file, from, to }]`. Look at a still of that moment at 100%: hair edges and anything held in front (a cushion, a mic) must read right. Without a cutout the text draws in front.
- **Anything else** (a drawing that draws itself, a custom chart, a new kind of motion): write it for this brand as `brands/<slug>/components/<name>.tsx` with `file_put` (a React component; `useCurrentFrame()` counts from its own start; only `remotion`, `react` and `src/` imports), export it from the video's `config.ts` as `export const components = { <name>: Component }` (import it from `"../../../../components/<name>"`), and place it with `layers: [{ at, to?, component: "<name>", props, rect, layer }]`. Log it with `log_append` as a library candidate so the team can make it a shared block.
- **Check it against the reference before the render:** `video_stills` with `times` at the moments of each component (the reference's dense-sheet and change-strip times, mapped to this video), then compare them next to the reference frames, component by component, and fix what differs. Name anything still not exact to the person before the render, never after.

**Many shorts from one long video** (podcast, talk, interview, webinar, livestream, a long brand video): the fastest path from one upload to many posts.
1. **Upload:** `media_upload_page` (files up to 5 GB; bigger: a compressed copy). One file is enough.
2. **Transcribe:** `media_transcribe` on it. It writes `<file>.lines.txt` next to the file: one line per stretch of speech with its times (`[12:03.4–12:15.9] text`). Read that with `file_get`, not the word list.
3. **Pick the moments:** each one a single idea that stands alone, 20–60 s (up to 90 s for a story), that ends on a payoff and doesn't need what came before. Prefer lines that make a viewer stop: a strong opinion, a number, a confession, a contradiction, a how-to, a story with a turn. Leave out stretches with another speaker talking over, or with bad sound. How many: what the person asked for; otherwise every strong moment (often 5–10 an hour). Give each an id, its times and its best line. Unless the person said to just go ahead, show the list once (title, times, the hook line) in the same message as the plan; that's the one stop.
4. **Cut them all at once:** `media_clips` with the source and the list (up to 12 per call; each clip becomes its own job and they all run at once, so `job_wait` each one). Each clip gets its own small file, its sound already cleaned (background noise removed, voice enhanced; `clean: false` keeps it as recorded) and re-timed words in `media/brands/<slug>/clips/<folder>/`, so nothing downstream touches the long file.
5. **One video per clip** in one campaign (for example `<date>-clips-<source>`), format `conversacion` for a person talking (it gives 9:16 and 16:9 from one config, drops pauses, copies the hook line to the start, adds name, captions with keywords in the accent). `config.ts`: `video` and `audio` are the clip's `.mp4` and `.wav` (paths under `media/`, written without it), `from: 0`, `to` the clip's length, `words` and `habla` imported from the clip's `.words.json` / `.habla.json` (`import words from "../../../../../../../public/brands/<slug>/clips/<folder>/<id>.words.json"`), `rotulo` with the speaker's name and role, `hook` on the clip's best line, `subtitulos.claves` for its key words. The config type is `ConversacionConfig` (`file_get library/formats/conversacion/code/timeline.ts`). A person on a creator brand: the brand lists them in `system.talent` and the clip uses their name and look.
6. **Check, render, deliver in batch:** stills at each clip's hook and one mid-point, all jobs started together. Fix what's wrong, then `video_render` every clip at once: renders over the organization's limit wait in line and start on their own. Then QA + audit per render, the creative review, `video_deliver` and the post copy for each. Report all of them at the end in one short list with the links.

**Memes and text formats:** format `meme`: beats with layouts (`text`, `full`, `split`) and text styles, media from the brand's own footage, stock or images the person brings (never a copyrighted meme template), a bed and a hit per beat.

## 4a. Decide the look first, then build everything from it

A video looks professional when one system runs through it, and amateur when each element was decided on its own. So before writing a single phrase or photo, decide the look, once, with reasons, and let the renderer enforce it (`src/components/look.ts`, `file_get` it; types in `scene-model.ts`).

1. **Measure what the look must answer to:** the footage's light and color (`media_stats` → `color`, or the shot colors in a reference's `analysis.json`), the brand's tokens (`brand.ts`), the pace (`words_per_s` from the transcript, cuts per minute), the platform, and the reference's measurements when there is one (its surround color, text case, whether text cuts in or animates).
2. **Derive, then reason:** in `config.ts`, `import { deriveLook, withLook } from "../../../../../../../src/components/look";` (the same depth as the brand import, one level higher), then `const look = withLook(deriveLook({ brand, footage: { Y, U, V }, energy: { wordsPerSecond, cutsPerMinute }, reference: { surround, textCase, cutsPerMinute, entrance }, platform }), { … })`. `deriveLook` writes a reason per choice; keep every reason you agree with and override only what the brief, the brand or the reference argues for, adding your reason to `reasons`. Never pick a preset by name: the look is derived from this video.
3. **Pick once:** when the person works with choices (autonomy `pick-from-top-3`), render stills of the same two moments under two or three looks (different palette, type or density, each derived and reasoned) and let them pick one. After that there are no more look questions: everything follows it.
4. **Build from the look, not from values:** `look` goes in the config; phrases take `look.type` and `look.palette` (write no sizes, fonts or colors per phrase unless a component of the reference demands it); photos, cards and grids take `look.photo` and `look.motion` (never a radius, shadow, tilt or entrance per item); entrances snap to the beat grid (`beats` from `media_beats`, on the output clock) when `look.motion.beatSnap`.
5. **Faces are facts:** `media_transcribe` on a video recording now also writes `<name>.faces.json`; import it as `faces` in the config (the format maps it onto the cut). With `look.layout.avoidFace`, every phrase moves off the face by itself, inside boxes too. Don't place text by eye on the face's side of the frame.
6. **The paper has a texture** (`look.palette.texture`): when the picture shrinks or shifts, flat paper reads cheap. `deriveLook` picks the footage itself, blurred and lifted toward the paper (`kind: "footage"`), so the surround belongs to the scene; `grain`, `linen`, `pattern`/`image` with a file the brand supplied, or `flat` when the reference or the brand asks for it. A layout's own `background` still wins when it's set to something other than `"paper"`.
7. **Motion and sound come from the look too** (`look.motion`, `look.sound`): a spring behind every settle and every word (`kinetic: "scale" | "reveal" | "none"`), one transition between layouts (`cut | wipe | push | whip | zoom`), a push-in on a key word at most every `punch.every` seconds, and effects from the library (`sound.sfx`: `enter` on each grid, `transition` on each layout change, `emphasis` only where a punch meets a chapter) planned and capped by the rulebook. `deriveLook` sets all of it from the pace: a calm piece gets soft pushes and revealed words, a fast one whips and springs. Change it with reasons, never per element. A music bed (`music: { file, volume }` in `creator`) is ducked under the speech from the transcript's words; the brand's style grades and textures the footage.
8. **Collages are grids:** `grids: [{ at, to?, items: ["<image>", { text }], style: "mood" | "masonry" | "polaroid" | "strip" | "feature", stagger?, order? }]`. The renderer lays the items out around the picture's box from the look (uniform treatment, consistent gaps, corners first, staggered on the beat). Use `media` only for a single sticker, clip or placed image, not for collages.

## 4. The edit

Write `config.ts` with `video_save` (`config`), copying the shape from an existing video of the same format (`video_get` on it) and the format's config type (`file_get library/formats/<id>/code/timeline.ts` or the format's manifest). The shape: `export const compositionId = "<BrandVideo>";` and `export const <name>: <Format>Config = { brand: <brand>Brand, voiceFile: voices.A.tight.file, voiceWords: voices.A.tight.words, voiceDuration: voices.A.tight.duration, musicFile: voices.post.musicFile, audioPost: voices.post, shots: [...] … }`, with `import voices from "../../../../../../public/brands/<slug>/audio/<video>/voices.json"` and `import { <brand>Brand } from "../../../../brand"`. For a recorded voice, import the `<name>.words.json` the transcription wrote the same way. Mark `punch: true` only on up to 4 shots that land on emphasis words. Footage push, color matching and title hold times come from the template. Text (fonts, sizes, colors, caption paging, placement, motion) follows the look the person chose or their reference (Text looks above); without one, the format's default. Cut on the voice: shots change on the words they illustrate (`at: { re: "^word" }`), and text appears on the word it repeats. The connector checks the config's shape and that it only touches this brand; a mistake in the code shows up as the stills job's error (read it, fix, retry).

**Canvas.** Videos are 9:16 (1080×1920) unless `config.ts` also exports `aspect`: `export const aspect = "16:9";` or a list, `export const aspect = ["9:16", "16:9"];`, which makes one composition per aspect from the same config. Presets: `9:16`, `16:9`, `1:1`, `4:5`. Jobs take `aspect` to pick one. `export const platform = "<id>"` overrides the audit platform.

**Re-edits (`reedit`).** Sound effects go in `sfx: [{ file: "brands/<slug>/…/sound.mp3", at: <output seconds>, volume: 0.35 }]`: set the volume from `levels.json` and listen in a proxy; effects with speech in them will show up in QA as unscripted. The audit measures where each line of an overlay lands and names the one that fails: start at `y` ≥ 288, end above y 1248, and stay out of the app's buttons on the right. Centred text can be about 696 px wide above y 840 and 480 px below it; for a wider line below y 840 use a fixed box on the left (`x: 120, w: 660`). Footage with text burned in collides with new overlays and no check sees it: take stills when each overlay appears and whenever the source shows text, then move the overlay to a clear area or cover the old text on purpose.

**Look before rendering:** `video_stills` (default 8 spread frames; ask for `times` at the hook, every card and the CTA), then `job_wait`: the contact sheet comes back as an image. Look at it: safe zones, text size, that frame 0 already shows the hook, that footage and text match the script. `video_proxy` (a 540p cut, `from`/`to` in seconds) for motion, timing and sound; give the person the link when their eyes help. A proxy is a half-size preview and its link lasts an hour: say so, and don't call the video finished until it has been rendered and delivered. Fix the config and look again; iterate here, not on final renders. After a fix, ask stills only for the `times` it touched. **You approve the cut:** the person approved the script in `plan`, so when the stills pass your look, render. Don't stop to ask them about stills or a proxy unless they asked to see one.

## 5. Render and the checks

1. **`video_render`** when the stills pass (`lufs: -14` for social; the default −16 is for long-form). It counts against the organization's monthly renders. `job_wait` returns the MP4 link, the file `name` and the report (frames, loudness, delivery checks); a report with blocking problems fails the job: read the error.
2. **`video_qa`** on that render: the transcript must match the script (dialect-aware) with no missing words and no unscripted speech, no voiceless gap over the script's limit (1.5 s unless planned silences raise it), no speech in unmuted footage, and the loudness on target. The sheet comes back as an image. Fix and re-render if anything fails.
3. **`video_audit`** on that render (start it right after `video_qa`; it waits for that QA on the server): the rulebook (safe zones, hook, pacing, music, captions, platform conventions). Fix every **block**. Fix **warn**s unless the brand has an override (`system.json → ruleOverrides` with a reason) or the concept needs them; then add an override with a reason rather than ignoring them. Read `audit.md` with `file_get` for the details.
4. **Creative review (blocks delivery: rule REVIEW-01).** Technical QA only proves the file is correct; it can't tell whether the video is good. Watch the proxy and the stills at every beat, read the transcript, listen for anything unplanned, and judge the render as a viewer against the concept. Write `review.md` with `video_save` (`review`, with `etags.review` from `video_get` when it exists): `render: <the render job's result name, e.g. a-antiacne.mp4; the audit compares exactly that>`, `verdict: pass|fix`, and an answer with what you saw for each:
   1. Does it feel like the concept (the premise line)?
   2. Does the look follow the footage and the story? Is the brand serving the piece or covering it?
   3. Does the voice fit the tone, and was it chosen for this piece?
   4. Does the script match the picture: room for the images, no line describing what's on screen?
   5. Anything unplanned: speech in the footage or another language, garbled text, artifacts, odd places, jumps? Is every planned sound layer actually audible (measured, not assumed)?
   6. The 3-second walk on the real render: where would a viewer leave?
   7. Does it look native to where it runs? Social (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) should look like the feed; a commercial can be cinematic. A social piece that looks like an ad fails this question.
   8. **The design critique (rulebook pack `design-quality`, DESIGN-01 to DESIGN-10; `file_get library/rules/design-quality.json`):** one look everywhere, hierarchy, nothing over a face, density that fits the pace, motivated motion, photos in the same light, legible on the footage, layouts that mean something, the side-by-side with the reference, and "would a top editor post it". Answer each with what you saw on the stills and the proxy, not with what the config says.
   Run this critique on the stills and the proxy **before** the final render, not only after: fix, look again, up to three rounds on your own. Anything that still fails on the render: fix it and review the new render. A passing review is all delivery needs: `video_deliver` reads `review.md` itself, so don't run `video_audit` again after writing it. Then show the person (the MP4 link); their reaction is the real verdict, and their corrections go into the brand's `writing.md` (for that brand) or to the team (for the playbook).

## 6. Deliver and log

`video_deliver` (or `/reech-studio-cloud:deliver`): it requires a passing QA and a non-blocking audit on that render (or `force: true` with the reason in the post), copies the MP4 and its report to `media/brands/<slug>/deliverables/<campaign>/`, writes the cover and the SRT captions and a `post.md` template into the video's `deliver/` folder, and appends the post line to `posts.jsonl`. Its result says whether `deliver/post.md` still needs the copy (`post.needsCopy`). Then write the post copy into `deliver/post.md` with `file_put` (a hook line that isn't the video's first line, the CTA, 3–5 hashtags) and remove every TODO. Give the person the deliverable links (`media_links` on the MP4, cover and SRT) and summarize the video in 2–3 lines.

**Human checkpoint, only for a brand's first video** (or after a brand-system change): ask the person to watch it once. Otherwise deliver.

Each aspect (9:16, 16:9…) is rendered, checked and delivered separately.
